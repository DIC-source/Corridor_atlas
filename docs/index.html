All layers live in MNI ICBM152 2009 space at 1 mm. Both panels draw from the same label volumes, so a colour block on the MRI and the matching shape in the illustration are the same voxels.

How to use

Click any structure, tract or artery in either view to open its details at the top of this panel. Click the MRI to move the crosshair; scroll or use the slider to page slices. Click a structure in the illustration to jump the MRI there. Use the Lesion tab to drop a sphere and list everything within 5 or 10 mm; use the Corridor tab to set an entry and target and see what the trajectory crosses.

Limits

Atlas tracts are group averages and do not show displacement, infiltration or oedema. Brainstem level boundaries are approximate axial planes, the STN is an ellipsoid at the published DISTAL-atlas centroid (±12, −13, −5.5) because the ATAG STN label is only a few voxels at 1 mm, and the substantia nigra is the ATAG label grown by 1 mm. CerebrA's “ventral diencephalon” label also covers the cerebral peduncles and midbrain tegmentum; it is split at the plane from the posterior commissure to the mammillary bodies, so only the part in front of that plane is called hypothalamus / subthalamic region, the peduncle (where corticofugal fibres run) is labelled separately, and the rest joins the midbrain. The arteries and the MRA image are population averages (Mouches & Forkert 2019), already in MNI space, covering the whole brain. Averaging across people keeps the large, consistent arteries sharp (ICA, circle of Willis, M1, A1–A2, pericallosal, P1–P2, basilar) but blurs distal branches whose course varies between people, so small cortical branches look faint or missing. The 3D vessel surface is drawn where at least 12% of subjects had a vessel. Branches are grouped by position: vessels outside the brain envelope (skull base, extracranial) form their own group, a box around the skull base forms the ICA and circle-of-Willis group, and the rest take the territory they run through. These grouped surfaces are now off by default. Named arteries (ICA, M1, M2, A1, A2–pericallosal, PComm, P1–P2, P3–P4, SCA, basilar, vertebral) are centerlines traced with a minimal-cost path along the highest vessel probability between anatomical landmarks, with calibre from the radius atlas. Veins and dural sinuses (superior and inferior sagittal, straight, transverse and sigmoid sinuses, vein of Galen, internal cerebral veins, basal veins of Rosenthal) are not in any reachable dataset: their courses are placed from the template's own geometry (inner brain surface, corpus callosum, third ventricle, midbrain) and are approximate. With “Cut at slice” on, the 3D model is cut at the current MRI level.
